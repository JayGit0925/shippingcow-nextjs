Here's the short answer: FedEx updates its ground fuel surcharge **every Monday**, and the rate is set from the **U.S. EIA national average on-highway diesel price published the prior Monday** — a built-in one-week lag. The surcharge is a percentage applied to your base transportation charge *plus most other surcharges*, and since **December 2025** the ground table has been running **above 22%**, an all-time-high band. If you ship heavy products, fuel is quietly one of the largest lines on your invoice — and the only one that changes weekly.

Below is exactly how the 2026 mechanism works and how to plan around it.

## The weekly mechanism

Three rules explain almost everything about the fuel line on your invoice:

1. **It resets every Monday.** FedEx publishes a new ground fuel surcharge percentage at the start of each week.
2. **It's indexed to diesel — with a one-week lag.** The rate comes from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) national average on-highway diesel price, but not this week's print: FedEx uses the price published the **prior Monday**.
3. **It applies to the whole stack.** The percentage is charged on base transportation **plus most other surcharges** — residential, additional handling, oversize. It compounds everything above it on the invoice.

The lag is the useful part. When diesel jumps, you have roughly one week before your surcharge follows. Watch the EIA Monday print and you know next week's fuel direction before FedEx publishes it.

## Where rates sit in 2026

Ground fuel surcharges crossed **22% in December 2025** and have stayed in that elevated band. At 22%+, a shipment with $15 of base-plus-surcharges carries $3.30+ of fuel — on every single package. Multiply by a few hundred shipments a month and fuel is often the second-largest controllable line on a heavy shipper's bill.

## Why heavy packages pay disproportionately

Because the percentage rides on the whole surcharge stack, and heavy or bulky shipments carry the tallest stack:

| Cost line | Light parcel | 50 lb+ bulky parcel |
|---|---|---|
| Base rate | low | high |
| Residential surcharge | applies | applies |
| Additional handling / oversize | rarely | frequently |
| **Fuel (% of all of the above)** | small | large |

A $6 parcel absorbs roughly $1.30 of fuel; a $25 heavy shipment absorbs $5.50+. Same percentage, very different dollars. Fuel is effectively a heavy-goods tax.

## What sellers can do about it

1. **Track the EIA print, not the FedEx page.** The EIA diesel number every Monday tells you next week's surcharge direction one week early — useful for timing promos and quoting shipping-inclusive prices.
2. **Shrink the base the percentage applies to.** Every dollar cut from base and accessorials — tighter packaging, fewer handling triggers, smarter zone injection — also cuts the fuel line automatically. Fuel savings are a free byproduct of fixing the rest of the invoice.
3. **Ask your 3PL how fuel is passed through.** Pass-through terms differ meaningfully between providers, and discounted fuel programs exist. On heavy freight the difference compounds week after week.
